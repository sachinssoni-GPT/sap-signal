A quiet war room at 02:00 changes how you think as an architect.

When a cutover has a 30‑hour single window and no Plan B, your mental model shifts from “we’ll recover” to “we must not create extra failure modes.” That changes priorities: check the interfaces first, lock down conversion mechanics, and map ownership of every failing component before the clock starts.

[YOUR STORY: a time an integration broke at go‑live because a team assumed a consumer would reconcile manually] — if you have a short, single window, that assumption becomes a showstopper. Treat distributed tester sign‑offs as useful only if each tester’s scope maps to a live severity and an on‑call owner.

Practical takeaway: on Monday after such a cutover, produce a cross‑map linking every interface, job and custom object to a named on‑call owner and an explicit rollback or compensation action. Then automate and rehearse the three highest‑risk interfaces.
